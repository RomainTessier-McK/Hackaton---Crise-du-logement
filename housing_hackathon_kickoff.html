Where in Montreal is rent eating the largest share of income, and who is most exposed?

Rents in the Montreal Census Metropolitan Area grew about 11 percent year-over-year in 2025 while incomes lag far behind. The 2021 Census already shows acute renter cost-burden across a third of Montreal neighbourhoods, and the picture has worsened since.

63.5%

Sources: StatsCan Census 2021 FSA profile (196 Montreal-area FSAs), CMHC Rental Market Survey 2025 Montreal CMA.

Three lenses on the same dataset

Geography, people, or policy. Pick the one that pulls you. These three map most cleanly to a real partner ask.

Geography
Where on the map is the pain, and how does it cluster?
  • • Which Forward Sortation Areas are most stressed, and how does the stress cluster spatially across the island and the suburbs?
  • • How well do the 23 CMHC rent zones align with the 196 Census FSAs, and where do the two sources disagree on what counts as expensive?
  • • Which neighbourhoods have both high rent and high cost-burden, separating real stress from high-income enclaves where rents are high but tenants can pay?
People
Who is being hit hardest, and where do vulnerabilities compound?
  • • Which demographics — one-parent families, recent immigrants, seniors, single-person households — concentrate in the most-stressed FSAs?
  • • Where does affordability stress compound with other vulnerabilities such as low-income share or limited language access?
  • • Is the renter-side cost-burden materially worse than the all-household measure our starter uses as a proxy?
Policy
What can be done, and where is the leverage biggest?
  • • Where is renter need highest but the subsidised-housing supply lowest? This is the most direct ask for a municipal partner.
  • • Where has rent grown fastest relative to income since the 2016 Census, and where is new construction not catching up?
  • • Which FSAs would the next ten units of social housing most reduce cost-burden in — the marginal-return question for the OMHM portfolio team?

For renters

Pitch 1 — STIR-Score Montréal

A pre-rent advisory tool. The renter types their postal code, monthly rent, and household income. The app returns their Shelter-cost-to-Income Ratio, the median across their Forward Sortation Area, and a templated landlord letter with comparable rents from nearby FSAs attached as anchoring evidence. The renter walks away with a single artifact they can actually send.

The data
Three of the six datasets already in the bundle do most of the work. The StatsCan 2021 Census FSA profile supplies the per-FSA median rent, median income, and cost-burden share that drive the badge. The CMHC Rental Market Report 2025, Table 1.1.2 supplies current asking rents by zone, mapped to FSA, so the comparison reflects 2025 not 2021. The free StatsCan Postal Code Conversion File (PCCF) turns the renter's six-character postal code into an FSA in one lookup, no geocoding required.
The actions
Inside the app the renter sees a real-time STIR badge (green under 30 percent, amber 30 to 40, red over 40), a what-if slider that previews a 10 percent rent shock or income shock, and a peer-FSA recommender that surfaces three nearby FSAs with similar transit access but lower median rent. The headline outbound action is a landlord letter PDF populated with comparable-FSA rents as anchoring evidence. Severity-red triggers a soft handoff to the nearest comité logement in the RCLALQ network with a pre-filled intake form.
Ship at hour 2
A Streamlit app that takes a postal code and income, prints the STIR badge, and lets the user download a landlord-letter PDF. Hosting on the free Streamlit Cloud tier counts as ship.

For Ville de Montréal and OMHM

Pitch 2 — Subsidy Gap Finder

A 2-D quadrant map that plots every Forward Sortation Area on stress (vertical axis) against subsidised-housing share (horizontal axis). The municipal housing portfolio manager can click into the four quadrants and see exactly where the existing subsidy footprint matches today's stress geography — and where it does not. The app emits a draft reallocation memo and opens a portfolio ticket directly into OMHM's workflow tooling.

The data
The pre-joined mtl_fsa_joined.geojson from gis_analysis.py contains polygons, stress, and subsidy share in one file — the entire X and Y axes of the chart are already there. To make the memo operational you add three external sources: the OMHM annual report (current subsidised-unit count per arrondissement), the SHQ Programme de supplément au loyer quarterly allocations, and the Ville de Montréal rôle d'évaluation foncière (vacant-parcel inventory) for the siting recommendations.
The actions
The user filters by quadrant and clicks any FSA to open a drawer with its 21 Census metrics, polygon outline, and top demographics. Each FSA carries a Gap Score equal to its stress percentile minus its subsidy percentile, ranging from minus 100 (over-served) to plus 100 (under-served). Outbound triggers are concrete: a 2-page reallocation memo briefing the OMHM executive on the top-five underserved FSAs, a portfolio ticket POSTed to OMHM's Jira or SHQ workflow tool, and a movement-digest email when any FSA's Gap Score shifts by more than 10 points after a CMHC quarterly release.
Ship at hour 2
A Folium map with the live quadrant filter, plus a Python function that emits the reallocation-memo .docx for the H3A through H3H downtown cluster. Use the existing GeoJSON. Jira integration is stretch.

For FRAPRU and the comités logement

Pitch 3 — Stress Cluster Alert

A community-organising dashboard that intersects renter stress with demographic vulnerability — one-parent families, recent immigrants, seniors — to surface the Forward Sortation Areas where economic stress is compounding with social fragility. The output is an operational artifact for the organiser: a canvassing brief per FSA, an assignment pushed to the volunteer roster, and a templated SMS blast to opted-in renters.

The data
Start with mtl_fsa_census_subset.csv and extend the KEEP_IDS list in analysis.py to pull the demographic characteristic IDs — one-parent family share, recent immigrant share (5-year arrivals), population aged 65 and older, and visible minority share. The mtl_fsa_joined.geojson handles the spatial filter. The Centraide of Greater Montreal vulnerability index is the public cross-check. The FRAPRU member-organisation SMS opt-in list is the operational connector.
The actions
The organiser picks one to three demographic dimensions and the dashboard live-recomputes a compound-vulnerability score per FSA. A ranked list and a map tinted by compound score surface the priority neighbourhoods. Outbound actions are where the app earns its keep: a one-page canvassing brief PDF per FSA (demographics, doorstep script in French and English), a canvassing assignment pushed to Airtable or Notion via REST API, a bulk SMS via Twilio to opted-in renters in the cluster, and a press-release draft when a new severe-stress and compound-vulnerability FSA crosses the threshold quarterly.
Ship at hour 2
A Flask plus Leaflet page that picks one to three demographic dimensions, renders the ranked list, and prints a canvassing brief PDF for the top FSA. Twilio integration is stretch.

For CMHC and market analysts

Pitch 4 — Rent Pulse Montréal

CMHC's Rental Market Report lags real-market rents by about two years. Rents in the Montreal CMA have grown roughly 30 percent since the 2021 Census reference year, but the official survey still anchors to that older baseline. This app closes the lag by pairing CMHC's October 2025 official rents with continuously-scraped asking-rent listings, and emits a real-time per-FSA lag heat-map. Outputs feed directly into the next CMHC Quarterly Housing Market Insights publication.

The data
The CMHC RMR Table 1.1.2 already in the bundle is the official baseline. The challenging part is the live-listing layer: Centris.ca via the Multiple Listing Service feed operated by the Québec Centris-affiliated real estate boards is the gold standard for comparables (requires partnership). Kijiji and Facebook Marketplace are the easier scrape, with FSA tagging via Nominatim geocoding. Inside Airbnb Montreal quantifies how much long-term housing has been siphoned to short-term rentals per FSA — useful as a supply-side adjustment.
The actions
The dashboard shows a weekly heat-map of the dollars-per-month lag (asking median minus CMHC official) per FSA, a time-series per zone with the CMHC anchor plus a rolling four-week asking-rent median, and an anomaly tracker for FSAs whose median asking moves by more than 5 percent week-over-week. The headline outbound is a weekly PDF brief auto-emailed to a CMHC analyst distribution list via GitHub Actions cron and SMTP. A secondary API push feeds CMHC's internal market-monitoring dashboard. Listings priced more than 50 percent above the CMHC zone median are auto-flagged to the Tribunal administratif du logement outreach team as potential illegal-rent-hike candidates.
Ship at hour 2
A scraper that pulls 100 Kijiji Montreal listings, geocodes each to FSA, computes the lag against CMHC Table 1.1.2, and emits a CSV plus a single heat-map PNG. The cron-emailed weekly brief is stretch.

For the Ville de Montréal Service de l'habitation

Pitch 5 — The Three-Way Bullseye

The most ambitious of the five pitches and the one that uses every dataset in the bundle. Join three layers — renter cost-burden, economic activity (where the tax filers are), and new construction (where the permits are flowing) — and rank Forward Sortation Areas by their three-way overlap. The bullseye condition is high stress + high economic activity + low new construction: workers are concentrated there, they cannot afford to live there, and supply is not catching up.

The data
Four datasets, all already downloaded. The pre-joined mtl_fsa_joined.geojson brings stress and demographics. CRA Individual Tax Statistics by FSA (Table 1a, 2021 tax year) brings the FSA-resolved economic-activity proxy: tax-filer counts and total income. The Ville de Montréal Permis de construction CKAN feed (1,661 aggregated records, 1990 through 2026) brings construction activity at arrondissement resolution. StatsCan Table 34-10-0156 (CMHC monthly housing starts) brings the CMA-level macro timing signal.
The actions
Per FSA the app computes three percentile ranks: stress, economic activity, and an inverted permit-volume rank (lower permits is worse). The composite bullseye score is the sum, capped at 3.0. Outputs are a CSV ranking of bullseye FSAs as direct input to the Ville de Montréal housing-portfolio review, a one-page diagnostic memo naming the top-five bullseye FSAs and their permit gap, and a tri-variate choropleth where bullseye FSAs glow red. This app is the natural front-end for the Subsidy Gap Finder (Pitch 2) extended with a third axis.
Ship at hour 2
The CSV ranking and the tri-variate choropleth, using the existing GeoJSON plus the CRA and permits files in the bundle. The full interactive front-end is stretch.

Ground rules
  • • Pair or trio is fine. Talk out loud and challenge each other's interpretations.
  • • Ship over polish. One clean chart beats five rough ones.
  • • Cursor is the tool. Lean on it for boilerplate so the time goes to the idea, not the syntax.

Stretch goals
  • • Build the FSA-level GIS choropleth. Boundary file and pre-joined GeoJSON are already in the bundle.
  • • Layer the CMHC 2025 rent shock as a forward-looking overlay on the 2021 Census picture.
  • • Pair affordability with a demographic intersection — one-parent families, recent immigrants, or seniors.
  • • Cross-link with Theme 4 (climate vulnerability). Heat-island times affordability is a real Montreal story.
